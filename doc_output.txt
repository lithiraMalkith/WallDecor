2. Project Overview & Objectives
Developer Instructions: Provide a concise summary of the project based on your research. Define what success looks like for this website deployment.

Executive Summary: Ceylon Wall Decor is a Sri Lankan small business that hand-makes textured wall art in standard sizes (6×6 to 20×24 inches) and custom sizes. It is promoted mainly on TikTok (@ceylonwalldecor) and sold through phone and WhatsApp. This project replaces manual, chat-based ordering with a custom-built e-commerce website using Next.js and Firebase. Customers get a searchable catalog, size and frame selection, custom-design upload and a simple Cash on Delivery checkout; the owner gets an admin panel to manage products, orders and customers. The site becomes the hub that TikTok and other social traffic lands on, with Meta and TikTok pixels to measure conversions, and it is architected so online payments can be added later.

Primary Website Goals:
Goal 1: Convert social traffic into online orders. A mobile-first storefront and COD checkout lets a TikTok viewer browse, choose design, size and frame, and place an order in about three minutes without waiting for a WhatsApp reply.

Goal 2: Centralise and automate operations. A Firebase-backed admin panel manages the catalog, prices, orders, custom-design requests and customer records, with automatic confirmation emails replacing manual chat records.

Goal 3: Build an owned, measurable and searchable brand presence. SEO-ready pages, Meta and TikTok pixel tracking and a trust layer (gallery, reviews, policies) reduce dependence on TikTok and prepare the business for future online payments.
Success Criteria: What success looks like for the deployment
Customers can complete an order end-to-end on a mobile phone using COD; the order appears in the admin panel immediately and a confirmation email is sent.
The full catalog (designs, sizes, prices, frame option, images) is managed from the admin panel without developer help.
Meta and TikTok pixels fire the ViewContent, AddToCart, InitiateCheckout and order events, verified in each platform’s diagnostic tools.
Mobile PageSpeed performance score of 90 or higher on the home, category and product pages (see Section 9).
Post-launch KPIs tracked from day one: website conversion rate, share of orders placed on the site versus WhatsApp, average order value and cart abandonment. Numeric targets to be agreed after 30 days of real data.

7.2 Core Workflows & Admin Access Control
Developer to outline how the administrative setup will handle different system roles (e.g., Owner vs. Staff privileges) and how an order will be managed inside the admin portal from the moment it is placed until it is marked completed.

Proposed User Roles & Restrictions: Role-based access control using Firebase Authentication custom claims:
Owner / Super Admin: Full access, including users and roles, settings, pixels, financial reports, deletions and order reversals.
Manager: Products, categories, orders, custom requests, customers, promotions and content; cannot manage users, roles or system settings.
Order Staff: View orders and move them through fulfilment statuses only (Processing to Dispatched); cannot edit prices, see revenue reports, export customers or delete anything.
Content Editor (optional): Products, media, FAQs and banners only; no access to orders or customer data.
How it is enforced: Roles are assigned only by the Owner. Access is checked in Firestore Security Rules and in server code, not only by hiding menu items in the interface. Admin sign-in uses email and password with optional two-factor authentication, session timeout and an activity log. Customers use guest checkout and never receive admin access.

Proposed Internal Order Workflow: Every status change records the time and the staff member who made it.
1. Pending: Order placed on the website (COD); admin alert and customer confirmation email sent.
2. Confirmed: Staff verify the order by phone or WhatsApp (address, size, frame); protects against fake COD orders.
3. Processing: The piece is being made or framed; ready-stock items are reserved.
4. Ready for Dispatch: Finished, quality-checked and packed.
5. Dispatched: Handed to the courier; courier name and tracking number saved; customer status email sent.
6. Delivered: Courier confirms delivery.
7. Completed: COD cash received from the courier and reconciled; order closed.
Exception statuses: On Hold, Cancelled, Delivery Failed and Returned. Steps cannot be skipped, and only the Owner can reverse or cancel an order after it has been dispatched.

8. Workflows & Core Integrations
Developer Instructions: Map out the system workflows ensuring compliance with the operational conditions below.
8.1 Order & Payment Workflow

Current Requirement (Cash on Delivery): The system supports a seamless COD checkout. No payment gateway is integrated in this phase.
Guest checkout form (name, phone, email, address, district) with phone-number and address validation.
The server recalculates prices, frame cost and delivery fee, then creates the order in a Firestore transaction with a unique order number (for example CWD-000123).
The order is saved with payment method “COD” and payment status “Pending – collect on delivery”; the stock check runs at the same time.
Abuse controls: reCAPTCHA / App Check, rate limiting, and phone confirmation by staff before production.

Future Scalability Configuration: The checkout and database are designed so a payment gateway can be added without restructuring.
The order schema already contains paymentMethod, paymentStatus (pending / paid / failed / refunded), amountPaid, transactionReference and currency, so no data migration is needed later.
Payment methods are built as pluggable adapters selected at checkout and switched on in admin settings; COD is the first adapter.
Adding a gateway (for example PayHere or another Sri Lankan provider) means: create a payment session on the server, redirect the customer, receive the gateway webhook, verify its signature, update paymentStatus and move the order to Confirmed automatically.
Order status and payment status are tracked separately, so the existing workflow does not change.
Secret keys stay in server-side environment variables or Secret Manager and never in browser code; refunds can be added to the admin Order module.
8.2 Automated Notifications Workflow

Email Notifications: Upon a successful order placement, the system must trigger an automated email notification to the customer. This should utilize a free transactional email service provider.

Proposed Free Email Service Provider: Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) free plan, using its transactional email API / SMTP. Alternative: Resend free tier. Free-tier sending limits (Brevo is roughly 300 emails per day) must be re-checked at setup; they are ample for the current order volume.
Emails: customer order confirmation (order number, items, sizes, total, COD note, address, contact details), admin new-order alert, status updates (dispatched with courier and tracking) and custom-request received / quote.
Trigger: a Firestore order-created event (Cloud Function) or Next.js server action calls the provider API; the API key is stored as a secret.
Deliverability: verify the sending domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC so emails do not land in spam.
Failures are logged and retried, the order is flagged in admin, and templates are editable in the admin panel.

8.3 Marketing Pixels (Critical for Social Traffic)
Facebook / Meta Pixel Integration: Tracks Page Views, Add to Cart and Purchase events.
Events: PageView, ViewContent (product page), AddToCart, InitiateCheckout and Purchase (fired when a COD order is successfully placed, with value, currency LKR and content IDs).
Meta Conversions API (server-side) with a shared event_id for de-duplication, to reduce data loss from iOS privacy features and ad blockers.
Pixel ID and access token stored in admin Marketing Settings; verification with Meta Events Manager and Pixel Helper.

TikTok Pixel Integration: Attributes video ad spend to conversions.
Events: ViewContent, AddToCart, InitiateCheckout and PlaceAnOrder / CompletePayment (mapped to “order placed”, since COD has no online payment; final mapping chosen with the client’s ad-optimisation goal).
TikTok Events API (server-side) with de-duplication event IDs; the ttclid and UTM parameters are captured and saved on each order for attribution.
A consent banner controls when the pixels load, and the Privacy Policy discloses tracking.

Firebase plan and cost: Cloud Functions and, for newer projects, Cloud Storage typically require the pay-as-you-go Blaze plan (which still has a free monthly quota). Budget alerts and efficient queries are needed to avoid surprise bills.
Firestore search and filtering: Firestore has no full-text search or relational joins; catalog search and multi-filter queries need planned indexes or a free-tier search add-on.
Security misconfiguration: Wrong rules could expose orders or allow price tampering or role escalation; mitigated by server-side pricing and rules testing with the emulator.
Fake COD orders and failed deliveries: Cost of wasted production and returns; mitigated by confirmation calls and, later, advance payment for custom orders.
Email limits and deliverability: Free-tier caps and spam-folder risk; mitigated by domain authentication and logging.
Tracking accuracy: In-app browsers, iOS privacy and ad blockers reduce pixel accuracy; server-side events and consent compliance are needed.
Performance versus rich media: Videos and animation can hurt PageSpeed.
Content readiness and rights: Poor photos or late assets delay launch; TikTok video reuse and customer photos need permission.
Platform changes: Meta and TikTok API or policy changes can break tracking.
Custom-order complexity: Quote-based pricing could grow the scope.
Data protection and lock-in: Storing customer personal data brings obligations under Sri Lanka’s data protection law; Firebase lock-in is reduced with a clean data-access layer.
